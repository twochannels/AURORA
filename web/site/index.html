AURORA

an ambient instrument for the M-VAVE FM-1

InstallPlay itSource

A custom firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1: big, wide sounds that keep moving on their own and bend to MIDI.

A pad of sine partials that tilts, spreads and drifts; a plate that goes on for ever, with a shimmer; a tape delay; and the Circuit Tracks' eight macros as eight named controls. A sound module for playing, not a groovebox: your sequencer sequences, AURORA sounds.

Early days. The spectral pad, the swarm, the air, the movement, the plate, the delay and the freeze are in; scenes are on the way.

What is in it

  • The pad. Eight voices of twelve sine partials, each with an ADSR envelope and velocity. TILT sets how fast the partials fall off, SPREAD balances odd against even partials from hollow to full, BRIGHT is a ceiling they fade under, BLOOM staggers their entry so a chord opens slowly, and DETUNE spreads each low partial into a slightly detuned pair, so the pad shimmers and the beats between a chord's notes are smeared into it rather than pulsing bare.
  • The swarm. Body under the pad: five saws a little apart, each wandering on its own, through a low-pass that tracks the note, opens with CUTOFF, darkens with TILT and bends with the movement and with pressure. LEVEL brings it in, from nothing to as loud as the pad.
  • The air. Noise, shaped: wind against crackle, through a resonant band-pass on each side whose colour you set with TONE and which wanders on its own at RATE. It swells in with the pad and lingers after the chord has gone, and it breathes on its own, in and out, slow and uneven, at the pace BREATH sets, or held steady at 0. AIR, the third macro, sets how much.
  • Movement. Four slow sources: WANDER, a random LFO living in every voice; WALK, a random walk; CHAOS, a Lorenz attractor; and TIDE, a slow sine over bars of the MIDI clock, or over minutes without one. They flow through a fixed pattern into TILT, COLOUR, PITCH and SPACE, each source's amount and each destination's depth a knob, MOVE scaling it all and RATE its speed. A random walk on every partial, DRIFT, on top. Nothing sits still unless you tell it to.
  • Width. Odd partials lean left, even right, by WIDTH. Mono-safe.
  • Space. A Dattorro plate with a shimmer at any interval and a tape delay that syncs to MIDI clock.
  • Freeze. FREEZE, the seventh macro, holds the delay line and plays a cloud of grains off it: GRAIN sets their length, SPRAY how far back they pick, SHIFT their pitch, an octave either way. The cloud goes on into the phaser and the plate, so the texture stays after the hands have left. Half way up it smears away over a minute; at the top it holds for ever, and new notes layer into it as much as the knob lets them.
  • The screen. Every page is a picture the four knobs shift, each part drawn in the colour of the knob that moves it: the spectrum on TONE, the ADSR on SHAPE, bloom, swell and velocity on TOUCH, the saws under their filter on SWARM, the wind on AIR, the sources' traces on MOVE, the matrix on SOURCES and TARGETS, the tail on SPACE, the repeats on DELAY, the grains on their line on FREEZE. HOME is the aurora itself, bent by the movement's own sources and lit by what you play, with TILT, SPACE, AIR and FREEZE under the knobs.
  • Presets. Twelve to start from, turned through with the PRESETS encoder or picked by program change: the slow and vast (DEEP, TIDE, CHOIR, ICE, STORM), the thick (SWARM, SUB), ORGAN, and the struck (GLASS, MALLET, PLUCK). HOME shows the name, starred once you have changed the sound.
  • Expression. CC 80 to 87, the Circuit Tracks' macro knobs, are MOVE, SPACE, AIR, TILT, WIDTH, DRIFT, FREEZE and BLOOM. Channel pressure or an expression CC swells the pad open. PLAY latches a drone with no hands on the keys.

Install

From the install page in Chrome or Edge, with the FM-1 on its USB cable, or from the FM-1 Firmware Hub. The rescue, holding OCT− at power-on, is always there, and the official firmware and any other custom firmware install over AURORA the same way.

In a dawless rig

Plug the sequencer's MIDI out into the FM-1's TRS jack (the FM-1 is TRS type A). Set the channel on the MIDI page, or leave it at OMNI. Hold chords from the sequencer, send the macros from its knobs, and let the movement do the rest.
